The Spider and the Fly are a stunning pair of neighboring star-forming nebulae and open star clusters in the constellation Auriga.
<!--more-->

Both are active stellar nurseries where massive, newly formed stars ionize the surrounding gas and dust clouds. 
They lie in the Milky Way's outer regions, in a part of the sky rich in star-forming nebulae.

## Spider and Fly Trivia
- **Sky Constellation:** Auriga (The Charioteer)

- **IC 417 — The Spider**
   - Contains a young open star cluster called **Stock 8** near its center. Its stars are estimated to be less than 3 million years old—an eye-blink in cosmic terms.
   - Measures about 26 light-years in diameter and lies approximately 7,000 light-years from Earth. However published estimates vary between 6,700 and 10,000 light years.
   - Spans a little less than half the Moon's apparent diameter, though it is vastly fainter.
   - Discovered by German astronomer Max Wolf on September 25, 1892.

- **NGC 1931 — The Fly**
   - Often described as a "miniature Orion Nebula" because of its compact structure and
   the fact that it features its own tight, compact "Trapezium" cluster of hot young stars
   carving out a glowing cavity.
   - Measures about 10 light-years across and lies approximately 7,500 light-years away.
   - Spans roughly one-fifth of the Moon's apparent diameter, although again it is vastly fainter.
   - Discovered by Englishman William Herschel on February 4, 1793.

## Imaging Details
Celestron RASA 8 telescope, ZWO ASI183MC camera, 439 × 30-second exposures stacked together to give 220 minutes of total integration time, captured October 8, 2026. Processed using Siril, Syqon and Affinity software.
The close-up images are crops of the full image and show the full resolution captured by the camera.
(This 3.7-hour exposure is longest I have taken and published up to now -- longer exposures capture more detail.)
